01 · molten dictation

Words land while you speak.

In the app, each word stays molten for at least 1.5 seconds, long enough for the recognizer to repair it in place, then freezes: a later guess never retypes it, though your own spoken edits can. Say “scratch that” to take back what you just said, “period” for punctuation, “new line” for a break, “new number” for a numbered list. Amounts, dates and phone numbers come out the way you'd write them — “twenty five percent by october sixth” types 25% by October 6 — and “emoji rocket” types 🚀. Misheard a name? “Spell that s i o b h a n” swaps the last word on screen, so “Shivon” becomes “Siobhan”, and “correct monday to friday” fixes a word anywhere in this dictation. Turn on caret commands, say one on its own — “select previous word”, “go to end of line”, “delete the line” — and the keyboard presses the real keys for it after your words have landed.

02 · registers

The same words, typed the way the app wants.

It checks which app has focus and picks a register: prose gets capitals and punctuation, and a terminal it recognizes gets digits and no sentence capitals. Map an editor to python, shell or javascript in [registers.map] and speech compiles into code: “for i in range ten colon” types for i in range(10):. The same check picks the keys: an editor gets ctrl← for “go back a word” (option← on a Mac), a Linux shell gets readline's altb. The panes show your last dictation through each register.

03 · your approval

Nothing starts until you ask.

The mic opens only when you ask: CtrlAltV (tap to start and stop, or hold to talk), or Right Ctrl or the on-screen button for Kai. Nothing listens in the background unless you turn on Kai's wake word, which is off by default. It types what you said, minus the “um”s and stutters (“the the”), and rewrites your words only when you say “VK, …” or switch on a setting that does (a style per app, tidying self-corrections). At a pause, rules (and, for an unclear one, your language model) may change the punctuation and capitals, never the words. A word you spell or correct is offered to your dictionary; nothing is learned until you accept it.

In a terminal it recognizes, it never presses Enter: a spoken “new line” types nothing, “VK, run …” (once you turn it on) drafts one line and stops, and no voice command presses Enter or a shortcut that acts like it (ctrl+m, ctrl+j, ctrl+o). Elsewhere “new line” presses what the field takes: Shift+Enter in chat apps and on most web pages, so a message doesn't send; nothing in a search box, a form field or a spreadsheet; Enter in documents, Google Docs included, and in other apps.

It goes by what it can see, and can guess wrong: a chat app it doesn't know gets Enter, which sends; a terminal it doesn't know (PuTTY, an editor's terminal panel, a shell in a browser tab) can run the line; on Windows a web form field or an address bar isn't seen as one line, so Shift+Enter can submit it; a web editor's own comment box gets Enter; and on macOS (beta) only the app's name counts. Name an app or a site in [registers.newline] to set its line breaks yourself.

04 · where your words go

Cloud speech with your key, or fully offline.

No speech model ships with the app. With a key for xAI (the default), OpenAI, Groq, Deepgram or AssemblyAI, your audio goes to that service while you dictate, and text you ask to hear goes to the voice service you choose. Point it at your own OpenAI-compatible speech server instead, such as Speaches, and your audio goes no further than that server. A language model handles rewrites, Kai and the punctuation at your pauses: xAI's Grok with an xAI key, or a local one such as llama-server. No analytics. Your recorded audio is held in memory while it is transcribed and never written to disk.

This page follows the same rules: no analytics, no cookies. After your first dictation your browser keeps the speech model in its cache (clear this site's data to remove it); nothing you say is stored. Its Content-Security-Policy lets it reach only this site, the speech model's hosts and our hosted engine. It fetches the model only once you tap the mic and allow it, and contacts the hosted engine only after you press a button in the sheet.

Install HyperFurion VK.

Download it and run it as yourself, not with sudo or as administrator. On Linux it asks for your password for the system steps (audio libraries, the virtual keyboard, adding you to the input group), and after the first install you log out and back in once. Setup then asks how to transcribe your speech — a key for a cloud service, or your own server (its hosted-service choice needs a subscription, which isn't on sale) — and about your hotkey, language, Kai (the push-to-talk assistant) and caret commands. Running a local llama-server? Setup finds it and offers its model as your language model.

What gets installed
  • Linux: a Python package in ~/.local/share/voice-keyboard-venv, the voice-keyboard CLI, a systemd user service that starts it when you log in, a GNOME Shell overlay extension (45–50), audio and clipboard libraries from your package manager, and the uinput rule for the virtual keyboard. It adds you to the input group, which the global hotkey needs; that group can read every keyboard and mouse.
  • Windows 10/11: a tray app with a native overlay and an on-screen Kai button. Installs Python for you if needed. It starts with Windows; turn that off with “Start with Windows” in the tray menu. Press CtrlAltV in any app; CtrlAltR reads a selection aloud.
  • Config: ~/.config/voice-keyboard/config.toml on Linux, mode 600. On Linux, and in classic Windows edit boxes, a password field forces the verbatim register and is never written to the history.

the full board

A keyboard you can talk to.

edit

Spoken edits

“Scratch that”, “new line”, “period”, “quote … unquote”, “new bullet”, “new number”, “new heading”, “new checkbox”, “emoji rocket” — a real edit grammar, parsed before anything is typed. Spanish, French and German sets are one setting away (“punto”, “à la ligne”, “neue Zeile”). Say literal to type the next word as-is.

numbers

Written the way you'd write it

“Twenty three” is 23 in a terminal and words in prose — until a unit makes it certain: 25%, $5.50, $3.2 billion, 3:30 PM, June 5, 1999, room 402, Q3, 2.5 MB, 555-1234, example.com/docs. Lowercase recognizers get their I, OK and Monday capitalized.

spell

“Spell that s i o b h a n”

Swaps the last word for the one you spelled — letters, NATO words, “capital k”, spoken digits — keeping its punctuation and its capital. Each fix is offered to your personal dictionary, never applied on its own.

navigate

Move the caret by voice

“Go left three words”, “select that”, “go to end of line”, “undo that”, “paste that”, “press tab”. Said on its own, a command presses the app's own keys after your words land — editors get the shift and ctrl chords, Linux shells get readline's. Enter is never one of them, and “paste that” works only where it can tell the app isn't a terminal. Off by default; one question in setup turns it on.

fix

“Correct monday to friday”

Fixes a word anywhere in this dictation, its capital kept, and offers the pair to your dictionary. “Cap that”, “uppercase that”, “lowercase that” recase the last thing you said — no model, no selection.

rewrite

“VK, make that formal”

Say “VK,” then an instruction, and what you just said goes with it to your language model and is repaired on screen, in place. Select a paragraph in an app first, and it rewrites the selection instead. On this page a fixed stand-in knows only formal, upper case and title case, and changes nothing for any other instruction; the app needs a language model (xAI's Grok with your key, or a local one).

assistant

Kai, in the keyboard

Hold Right Ctrl and ask. Ask for a command in a terminal, or in an app it can't identify, and Kai types a draft and leaves Enter to you ([assistant] terminal_fallback = false keeps an app it can't identify answer-only); anything else it answers aloud. It runs on your language model (a one-gigabyte local model can draft the commands). Kai is on by default and keeps your questions and its answers on your computer until you turn its memory off.

read

Read anything aloud

Select text and press your read-aloud key (CtrlAltR on Windows; on Linux, a desktop shortcut you bind to voice-keyboard tts) to hear it in xAI's voice, OpenAI's, ElevenLabs', or a local one.

recall

An opt-in memory

A local ledger of what you dictated: list it, re-type it, search it. It also finds words you re-dictated to fix them and offers each pair to your dictionary; nothing is applied until you accept it. Off until you turn it on.

the edges

Known limits.

  • No speech model ships with the app: you need a key for a speech service or your own speech server.
  • Words land while you speak with xAI, or with your own server at a local address (localhost, a private IPv4 address or a .local name), re-transcribed every 2.5 seconds; with OpenAI, Groq, Deepgram or AssemblyAI, or a server anywhere else, they land when you stop talking.
  • Linux and Windows are first-class. macOS is in beta, installed from a checkout. iOS lets no app press keys in other apps, so this one can't run there.
  • Repairs assume nothing else edits the field while you dictate. If focus moves to another app mid-sentence, typing stops there and the whole transcript goes to your clipboard, replacing what was on it.
  • A caret command needs a pause before and after it; said mid-sentence it is typed as words. Terminals have no selection to extend. Caret commands ([nav]) and “VK, run …” ([intent]) are off by default in the app; this page has both on, with your own platform's keys. Full list on GitHub →

Use the hosted engine

By default this page recognizes speech with the model in this tab, and none of your audio leaves it. Nothing in this sheet sends anything until you press a button here: “check the relay” asks api.hyperfurion.com, our relay, whether the hosted demo is up. After that, the switch sends your next dictation's audio through the relay to xAI's speech engine, the way the app sends audio to a provider you configure, then switches itself off; “ask” sends your question to xAI's Grok; “hear it” has xAI's voice read the answer aloud. The relay keeps no audio and no text, and counts each IP address's requests per day to share a small daily budget. The page's footer counts each of these as it happens.
