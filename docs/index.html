01 · molten dictation

Words land while you speak.

Each word stays molten for 1.5 seconds — long enough to repair itself in place — then freezes and is never touched again. Say “scratch that” to take a sentence back, “period” for punctuation, “new line” for a break. Misheard a name? “Spell that n g i n x” swaps the last word on screen. Say a caret command on its own — “select previous word”, “go to end of line”, “delete the line” — and the keyboard presses the real keys for it after your words have landed.

02 · registers

The same words, typed the way the app wants.

It probes the focused app and picks a register: prose gets capitals and punctuation, a terminal gets digits and no caps, python and shell compile speech into code. The same probe picks the keys: an editor gets ctrl← for “go back a word” (option← on a Mac), a shell gets readline's altb. The panes show your last dictation through each.

03 · consent

Only a hand may send.

In a terminal it drafts one line and stops. It never presses Enter to send — enforced in the injector, on every path, not promised in copy. No caret command can press it either: a shortcut that would (ctrl+m, ctrl+j) is refused before it reaches the keys.

The machine may draft. Only a hand may send.

04 · private by default

Your machine. Your keys. Nothing phones home.

Point it at a local OpenAI-compatible server and it runs fully offline. Or bring a key for xAI, OpenAI, Groq, Deepgram or AssemblyAI. Zero analytics, ever. Audio is held in memory while transcribed and never written to disk.

This page keeps the same rule: no analytics, no cookies, no storage, and a Content-Security-Policy that forbids every request except this site, the speech model when you tap the mic, and our hosted engine if you .

What gets installed
  • Linux: a Python package in ~/.local/share/voice-keyboard-venv, the voice-keyboard CLI, a systemd user service, a GNOME Shell overlay extension (45–50), and the uinput rule for the virtual keyboard. Your password is asked once, for the audio libraries and keyboard access.
  • Windows 10/11: a tray app with a native overlay and the Kai orb. Installs Python for you if needed. Press CtrlAltV in any app.
  • Config: ~/.config/voice-keyboard/config.toml, mode 600. Password fields always force the verbatim register and are never logged.

the full board

Everything a keyboard does, spoken.

edit

Spoken edits

“Scratch that”, “new line”, “period”, “open quote” — a real edit grammar, parsed before anything is typed. Say literal to type the next word as-is.

numbers

Digits where they belong

“Twenty three” becomes 23 in a terminal and stays words in prose. “One two seven” is 127. Decimals too.

spell

“Spell that n g i n x”

Swaps the last word for the one you spelled — letters, NATO words, “capital k”, spoken digits — keeping its punctuation and its capital. Each fix is offered to your personal dictionary, never applied on its own.

navigate

Move the caret by voice

“Go left three words”, “select previous word”, “go to end of line”, “press tab”. Said on its own, a command presses the app's own keys after your words land — editors get the shift and ctrl chords, shells get readline's. Enter is never one of them.

rewrite

“VK, make that formal”

The wake word sends what you just said through your language model and repairs it on screen, in place. Hold it for approval if you like.

assistant

Kai, in the keyboard

Hold Right Ctrl and ask. In a terminal Kai drafts the command and stops. Anywhere else it answers aloud. A one-gigabyte local model is enough.

read

Read anything aloud

Select text in any app and the keyboard speaks it — xAI, OpenAI, ElevenLabs, or a local voice.

recall

An opt-in memory

A local ledger of what you dictated: list it, re-type it, search it. It also mines your corrections to learn your vocabulary. Off until you turn it on.

the edges

Honest about the edges.

  • Linux and Windows are first-class. macOS is in beta, installed from a checkout. iOS cannot host a system-wide keyboard like this.
  • Live, molten typing needs a streaming speech provider. Non-streaming providers type when you stop talking.
  • Repairs assume nothing else edits the field while you dictate. Click elsewhere mid-sentence and the text lands on your clipboard instead.
  • A caret command needs a pause before and after it; said mid-sentence it is typed as words. Terminals have no selection to extend. Hands-free navigation is off by default in the daemon ([nav] enabled = true, or say yes in voice-keyboard setup); this page has it on, with your own platform's keys.
  • A spoken “new line” in prose types a line break. The Enter refusal applies where it matters: command drafting and Kai. Full list on GitHub →

Use the hosted engine

By default this page recognizes speech on your own machine and sends nothing anywhere. If you turn this on, your next dictation's audio goes to api.hyperfurion.com — our relay — and on to xAI's speech engine, exactly as the daemon would send it to a provider you configure. The relay keeps no audio and shares a small daily budget with everyone. The footer will count every request.
